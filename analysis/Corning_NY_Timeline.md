# Timeline of Corning, N.Y. — As Documented in the Local History Photo Archive

This timeline is derived from 1,977 cataloged photo descriptions in the Corning area local history collection. Only 589 records carry dates; the remaining 1,388 are undated. Approximate dates (e.g., "c. 1900") are noted as such. Events are grouped into eras for readability.

---

## Pre-Civil War (1842)

**1842** — The earliest item in the collection is an advertisement for **Corning Academy**, announcing a winter term beginning December 5, with tuition rates and course offerings. Leadership listed as Principal E. R. Cornell and Preceptress Amanda P. Robinson, signed by H. Bostwick (President of the Board of Trustees) and H. H. Hull (Secretary). This predates the incorporation of the Village of Corning (1848) and documents one of the area's earliest educational institutions.

---

## Civil War Era (1860–1867)

**1860** — A patriotic envelope bearing a portrait of General Winfield Scott with eagle and American flags, addressed to Flemingsburg, Ohio. Indicates the reach of Union propaganda materials circulating in the region at the war's outset.

**1861** — A handwritten document from the Town of Caton details municipal expenses, including clerk and election fees totaling $6.00. Early local government record-keeping at the township level.

**1862** — A military discharge document from Benton Barracks discharges Private William McMurden of the 1st Regiment of Pennsylvania. Civil War service by area residents is documented.

**1863** — Four items document the war more directly: a commission from Ohio Governor David Tod appointing Samuel Boydman as First Lieutenant in the U.S. Cavalry; a framed display pairing a Civil War-era American flag with the governor's commission; a photograph of **Camp Brough, Virginia**, showing canvas tents among bare winter trees; and a commemorative medal for the Battle of Chickamauga (dedication ceremony September 18–20, 1868).

**1860s (general)** — Artifacts include a Civil War-era bayonet and a combination fork-spoon utensil with a deteriorated metal handle — likely soldier's field equipment.

**1867** — A **Canal Revenue Certificate** from the New York State Canal Department, a $100 loan certificate bearing 6% interest, signed by N.S. Benton. Reflects the state canal system's role in the regional economy.

---

## Late 19th Century (1870–1899)

**1870** — Two records from **Cedar Street**: a photograph of a wooden residential building with white picket fencing and a property abstract/deed for 189 Cedar Street detailing ownership of the Williams Estate (lots 15 and 16 in Block 57).

**1880** — A **covered bridge** spanning a rocky streambed, surrounded by bare trees. One of the few records of covered bridge infrastructure in the Corning area.

**1885** — A two-story wooden house with front porch in **Caton, N.Y.** Period domestic architecture.

**1889** — Flood damage documented: industrial and residential buildings damaged, debris-strewn ground, standing water, railroad tracks in foreground. Associated with the Alcott Ron & Co. area. This is the earliest flood documentation in the collection and predates the better-known 20th-century floods.

**1891** — A woman from the Isabel Walker Drake family photographed in a snowy winter landscape, rural buildings in background.

**1892** — A holiday advertisement for **J. B. Collins & Co.** on East Market Street, promoting holiday gift stock with an illustration of Santa Claus.

**1893** — The **Erie Railroad Depot at Painted Post**, a wooden station building with tower alongside railroad tracks and telegraph poles. Documents the railroad infrastructure that defined the region.

**1895** — Two photos: a formal group portrait of ~14 students and faculty at **Corning Free Academy**, and a team photograph of the **Corning Base Ball Club** with identified players including Murtaugh, Whitehead, Boyle, Cobb, Friel, Crossley, Mahoney, Goodall, Harmon, and Clark.

**1896** — A **major fire** devastates part of Corning, leaving extensive rubble and charred debris. Also documented: theater posters advertising silent films and stage productions ("The Corsican Brothers," "David Garrick," "Monte Cristo") with admission prices of 20, 30, and 50 cents.

**1897** — A street view showing a church with distinctive spire, utility poles, and pedestrians. Also: the Drake family with a horse-drawn carriage at Penn Yan, N.Y. (dated July 26, 1897).

**c. 1900** — The largest cluster in the collection: 151 photos (split between "c.1900" and "c. 1900" due to inconsistent cataloging). Dominated by the **Isabel Walker Drake Family Photo** collection. Subjects include domestic life, residential architecture (Victorian houses with turrets and wraparound porches), street scenes, churches, and social gatherings. The Drake family appears to have been prominent in Corning during this period.

---

## Turn of the Century and the Progressive Era (1900–1919)

**1900** — A hot air balloon tethered in a field near the river draws spectators. Arthur C. Arthur identified as the manager of the **"Crystal City" Opera House**. The **Alliance Hook & Ladder Co.** fire department band poses with instruments in front of a stone firehouse. A Corning baseball team of ~16 men photographed.

**1901** — **Flooding** documented: a flooded street with wooden commercial buildings partially submerged, brick industrial buildings in water. Also: a brick commercial building with "Hardware" signage associated with Ferguson's Drug Store.

**1903** — Railroad scenes including steam locomotives (No. 1084), railway cars, a horse-drawn cart, and a creamery/dairy supply company building.

**1906** — The **Corning Glass Works** facility photographed: multiple tall industrial smokestacks emitting smoke, brick factory buildings. Two views document the scale of the glassmaking operation.

**1907** — The **Isabel Walker Drake family home**: a Victorian house with curved turret, wraparound porch, and large trees. Also: a group of ~13 women at Corning Free Academy.

**1908** — **First Street east from Pine Street**: a tree-canopied residential street with brick buildings and a horse-drawn carriage in the distance.

**1909** — The **City Hall and Fire Department** building: a Victorian structure with turrets and pointed spires. The **City Club** building, heavily covered in ivy. A baseball game in progress with spectators and a hillside backdrop.

**1910** — Seven photos. **Denison Park**: a scenic arched bridge over a pond with manicured landscaping. The **Episcopal Church**: a stone Gothic Revival structure with spired tower. A CFA baseball team portrait ("CFA 09-10"). A theatrical production with costumed performers.

**1910–1917 (various ranges)** — The **Ellsworth Cowles Collection** dominates this period: ~20 records of archaeological artifacts including stone tools, arrowheads, projectile points, and pottery from sites at Spanish Hill, Lamoka Lake, Chemung River flats, Tioga Point Farm, and others. Artifact types documented include Lamoka variants, bare island types, scrapers, drills, reamers, strike-a-lights, and blow gun points. These represent a systematic archaeological survey of the Chemung Valley and surrounding region.

**1911** — Ten photos. The **Erie Depot** with a steam locomotive at platform and gathered crowds. A **Chemung River** industrial scene with steel truss bridge and factory smokestacks. Courthouse Park with mature trees. Elevated views of the Northside neighborhood. Judson Badgley and the Electric Company. Hiram Coles and Roscoe Cram in Caton.

**1912** — Fourteen photos, notable for the **Delaware, Lackawanna and Western Railroad trainwreck near Gibson, N.Y.** — derailed and destroyed cars, casualties covered in white sheets, onlookers at the scene. Multiple views document this disaster. Also: the **First Congregational Church**, a brick Gothic Revival structure with tall tower.

**1913** — First Street viewed from Pine Street: Victorian houses, white picket fences, a dirt road. Two women in an automobile with American flags in Caton. A formal gathering at Denison Park's octagonal gazebo.

**1916** — The **clocktower at Centerway Square** with brick commercial buildings, utility poles, and industrial smokestacks. The **Erie Railroad Station in Painted Post** (brick, single-story). Class photos from CFA.

**1917** — The **Corning Fire Department** with early motorized fire trucks, firefighters in uniform at their brick station.

**1918** — Thirty-five photos — the second-largest single-year cluster. Almost entirely **World War I portraits** labeled "Corning's Part in the World War 1918." Subjects include Robert Austin, Thomas J. Cunningham, John H. Dougherty, and members of the **"Four Minute Men"** (wartime public speakers). Named subjects include Leo Glen Brown, Donald Tracy Craver, N. J. Allen, Edward W. Fitzpatrick, and Earl E. Burley.

**1919** — Two catalog pages of **glass cullet products** from Corning: hand-drawn sketches of goblets, champagne saucers, parfaits, bowls, and iced tea glasses with item numbers and prices. Documents the Glass Works' product line.

## Interwar Period (1920–1939)

**1920** — A class photograph of ~30 students at a brick building.

**1920s** — Rural life in Caton: horse-drawn wagons, hay loading, men beside automobiles. The automobile's arrival in the rural hinterland.

**1921** — Nineteen photos, almost all from **Painted Post** in March 1921 — a systematic photographic survey. Streets documented include Fenderson, First, Charles, Brewer, and Delaware Ave. Also: the DL&W Railroad tracks looking westerly. Unpaved roads, bare trees, utility poles, railroad crossings.

**1922** — A football team on the field with a banner reading "WATCH US GO SMILIN THROUGH."

**1923** — CFA class portraits and team photos: a football team ("CFA" banner), a women's baseball team in sailor-style uniforms. A woman at a mobile house trailer in Caton.

**1925** — A one-story white wooden schoolhouse in Caton.

**1927** — Students at a Caton schoolhouse.

**1928** — Multiple CFA team and group portraits: baseball teams in "C" uniforms, ~30 people on steps of a brick building with arched entrance.

**1930** — Female typists at the **Corning Leader** newspaper office. ~40 CFA faculty and staff.

**1933** — Twenty-two photos. Extensive documentation of the **Bridge Street Viaduct**: tree-lined street, automobiles, streetcar tracks, commercial storefronts (Standard Cleaning Co., "Old Gold" signage). Multiple angles of the viaduct as elevated bridge structure. Also: CFA and Ellsworth Cowles Collection entries.

**1934** — Five more **Bridge Street Viaduct** views: brick-paved street, white bollards, automobiles, "Louie's" dry cleaning, church steeple visible from the bridge.

**1935** — **Flooding on July 8, 1935**: houses submerged and displaced, the Corning and Painted Post Road underwater, High Street in Painted Post flooded. Also: a Victorian residential street (white house with turret, "LEE" street sign).

**1936** — Three Corning firefighters with a vintage fire truck at an ivy-covered firehouse with American flags.

**1937** — Flood damage at **Jamney Creek** — debris and wreckage.

---

## World War II Era and Postwar (1940–1959)

**1940** — Three people at an airfield in front of an American Airlines aircraft. A classroom group of ~20 elementary students.

**1940s** — A massive oversized light bulb structure, likely the **Edison Monument** in Corning. The **Deluxe ice cream shop** storefront downtown with the Ward Williams store adjacent.

**1944** — A teacher and ~9 female students in an art classroom at CFA, chalkboards with geometric drawings visible.

**1945** — **Bowers Creamery** in Caton (May 8, 1945 — V-E Day): a small industrial building with tall chimney in a rural landscape. Archaeological artifacts from a burial cache at the Perispino Basement site near Painted Post.

**1946** — Twenty-one photos. The **1946 Corning flood**: extensive interior damage at **Corning Glass Works**, including the flooded basement of Office Building 421, filing cabinets submerged in the Advertising Department of Building #21, flooded alleys with debris. Exterior views show flooded streets with 1940s automobiles submerged near brick industrial buildings. Mrs. Bebout at the Glass Works telephone switchboard is also documented. Additionally: a CFA football team, and Caton scenes.

**1947** — The **city incinerator** facility: industrial structures, metal fencing, residential buildings in background. Municipal infrastructure documentation.

**1948** — General **Dwight D. Eisenhower** at the **World War Memorial Stadium** dedication ceremony: three photos showing Eisenhower in military uniform at the microphone, smiling and gesturing, with First Army insignia visible. Crowds in attendance. (Eisenhower was then president of Columbia University, four years before his presidential election.)

**1950s** — A 1952 CFA football team. A 1953 **major fire** with firefighters on extended ladders battling a blaze in a brick building. Children exiting an Express bus at the **Armory**. CFA basketball team (1954) in "C" uniforms. The **Carder School** cornerstone. The **Baron Steuben Hotel** dining room (1956), formally set. The **clock tower** in 1956, surrounded by Victorian buildings and smokestacks. Aerial view of the **Corning Fibre Box Corporation** factory. A winter scene in Caton at a Route 225 junction (1958).

---

## The 1960s and Early 1970s

**1960s** — CFA students at a school event or assembly.

**1963** — **Dann's Dairy**: corrugated metal barn, "Dann's Dairy" sign, water tower.

**1967** — Aerial view of a Caton farmstead. Archaeological test hole artifacts from South Waverly, PA.

**1970** — A white wooden church in Caton in winter. The **Corning Free Academy** building facade: brick clock tower with clock face, arched windows.

**1971** — Nine photos systematically documenting **one-room schoolhouses in Caton** — small wooden structures, some with boarded windows, overgrown vegetation. An apparent survey of these buildings before they were lost.

---

## Hurricane Agnes Flood (1972)

**1972** — One hundred twenty photos — by far the largest single-year group, comprising 6% of the entire collection. The **Hurricane Agnes flood of June 1972** devastated Corning and is documented extensively:

- A white church surrounded by floodwaters with submerged farmland
- Houses damaged and displaced by flood debris
- The **Corning Glass Center** directional sign amid submerged vehicles near Jones Pontiac Co.
- Flood damage at the **Corning Glass Works**: industrial trucks submerged, sediment and debris
- The **Centerway Bridge** area
- **Bonady's Market** affected
- The **Northside** neighborhood
- The **Corning Fibre Box Corporation** facility
- Residential destruction across the city

The 1972 flood, caused by the remnants of Hurricane Agnes, was the most catastrophic natural disaster in Corning's history. The Chemung River crested well above previous records. The scale of documentation — 120 photos from this single event — reflects the magnitude of the disaster and the community's impulse to record it.

---

## Post-Flood (1973–1975)

**1973** — Caton scenes: a snow-covered blacksmith shop, an aerial view of the hamlet, a rural house.

**1975** — The Isabel Walker Drake family home revisited: a Victorian house with period automobiles parked in front. The **Crooker Farm**: fallen logs, a wooden rail fence, a stacked stone wall in wooded areas. The last dated entries in the collection.

---

## Notes on the Collection

**Dominant themes**: Flooding recurs across the full span of the collection (1889, 1901, 1935, 1937, 1946, 1972), making it the single most prominent thread. The Chemung River's periodic destructive floods are the defining natural force in Corning's documented history.

**The Isabel Walker Drake Family** (150+ photos, mostly c. 1900) provides the richest window into turn-of-the-century domestic and social life. The family was apparently well-positioned in Corning society.

**The Ellsworth Cowles Collection** (130 photos) documents a decades-long archaeological survey (roughly 1900–1960) of Native American sites across the Chemung Valley, Tioga Point, Spanish Hill, Lamoka Lake, and adjacent areas. Artifact types span thousands of years of pre-contact habitation.

**Corning Glass Works** appears throughout as the industrial anchor of the community — from 1906 smokestacks to 1946 and 1972 flood damage.

**Corning Free Academy** is documented from the 1840s through the 1970s — class photos, sports teams, building views — making it the longest-running institutional subject.

**70% of the records are undated**, which limits the timeline's completeness. Many undated photos from series 77 (195 photos) appear from their descriptions to depict early 20th-century downtown streetscapes, residential architecture, and commercial buildings that could fill gaps in this chronology if dated.
